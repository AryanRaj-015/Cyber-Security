# Activity: Cybersecurity Risk Register

- **Course:** Google Cybersecurity Certificate - Course 5 (Assets, Threats, and Vulnerabilities)
- **Platform:** Google Skills
- **Category:** Risk Assessment and Prioritization
- **Materials reviewed:** Risk register worksheet, completed analysis, and course exemplar

## Objective

Identify risks to a bank's assets, describe the vulnerabilities that could lead to incidents, and prioritize response using likelihood and severity ratings.

## Scenario

The exercise describes a coastal bank with on-premises and remote employees, individual and commercial customers, and strict obligations to protect customer data and funds. The operating environment includes physical, information, and third-party risks.

## Method

1. Reviewed the operating environment and identified the assets and risks listed in the worksheet.
2. Described the vulnerability associated with each risk.
3. Assigned likelihood and severity scores from 1 to 3.
4. Calculated each priority score as **likelihood × severity** and compared the resulting register with the course exemplar.

## My Risk Register

| Asset | Risk | Vulnerability or scenario | Likelihood | Severity | Priority |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Funds | Business email compromise | An employee is tricked into sharing confidential information. | 3 | 2 | **6** |
| Customer database | Compromised user database | Customer data is poorly encrypted. | 3 | 3 | **9** |
| Financial records | Data leak | A database server containing backed-up data is publicly accessible. | 2 | 3 | **6** |
| Funds | Theft | The bank's safe is left unlocked. | 1 | 3 | **3** |
| Business operations | Supply-chain disruption | Natural disasters delay deliveries. | 1 | 2 | **2** |

The customer database risk received the highest score in my assessment. Risk ratings are estimates based on the scenario; organizations should document assumptions and revisit scores as conditions change.

## Recommendations

1. Prioritize protection of customer data: restrict access, encrypt sensitive information, and verify database and backup exposure settings.
2. Reduce business email compromise risk with strong authentication, employee awareness, and clear reporting procedures.
3. Add access controls and monitoring for financial records and backup systems.
4. Protect physical funds with secure storage and documented access procedures.
5. Plan for supplier and delivery interruptions through continuity planning and alternative arrangements.
6. Reassess likelihood and impact with stakeholders and update the register when assets, threats, or controls change.

## Result

The register makes assumptions and relative priorities visible. In this assessment, weak protection of customer data has the highest priority, while business email compromise and publicly accessible backups also merit timely remediation.

## Key Takeaway

A risk register turns broad concerns into actionable priorities by connecting assets, vulnerabilities, likelihood, and impact. Scores support decision-making but should be reviewed alongside business context and evidence.
